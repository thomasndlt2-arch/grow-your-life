Rules for visits and invitations

Last updated: 14 October 2026

This is a place for care and respect. These rules apply to chosen names, gardens open to visitors, visits, gifts of bees and invitations. The service is run by Thomas Nadalet (Gusela Solutions), at the address and contacts below. Version: 14 October 2026.

1. What we ask of everyone

  • Choose a respectful name or nickname. Do not publish contact details, addresses, links or personal data about yourself or others, and do not impersonate another person.
  • Insults, discrimination, hate, harassment, threats, sexual content, child exploitation or abuse, incitement to violence and other illegal content are not allowed. This also applies to symbols, images or messages made with plants, paths and garden decorations.
  • Do not use visits, bees or invitations to harass, deceive, advertise or send persistent, unwanted messages. Do not bypass blocks, suspensions or checks to obtain rewards abusively.

There is zero tolerance for this content and behaviour. Thomas reviews the circumstances and chooses a proportionate response: a filter mistake does not automatically mean permanent suspension.

2. Age and participation

Visits and invitations are available from age 13. Before opening visits or choosing a name, bringing bees for the first time, preparing or sharing an invitation or confirming a code, the app asks you to accept the “Rules for visits and invitations”: “Accept and continue” accepts and continues; “Not now” leaves things as they are. Acceptance covers visits and invitations once per version on this phone. The version, date and point of acceptance stay only on the phone, outside the garden copy: a new version of the rules or another phone requires acceptance again. Reading the rules, reporting, blocking and unblocking do not require acceptance. Your personal garden remains available without visits and invitations.

The age question offers four choices, with none selected in advance: “Under 13”, “13 to 15”, “16 or older”, “I'd rather not say”. Where available, Apple’s age range also applies, without a date of birth; if the sources disagree, the more protective range applies. With “I'd rather not say”, only what Apple confirms applies; without a usable range, visits, invitations and analytics remain off. Under 16, you are not asked for analytics consent. You can correct your answer in “Settings” ▸ “Privacy” ▸ “Your age”.

Invitations are optional: share them only with people who want to receive them. Rewards do not require purchases, reviews or public posts. Any discount terms and eligibility are shown in the app and checked by Apple.

3. Filtering and blocking

The app checks names on your phone, without sending them to a filtering service, before publication and display. It uses all six languages’ lists together and also looks for disguised words, links, email addresses, phone numbers and control or invisible characters. It may miss abuse or reject a harmless name: “Choose another name” offers “Ask for a check” to email Thomas. A rejected incoming name appears as “Name not available”, including for VoiceOver; hiding a name does not by itself remove bees from a valid gift. The filter does not check garden designs or replace review of reports.

Find attribution and links in the app under “Settings” ▸ “About” ▸ “Licenses”, with “Source of the lists” and “CC BY 4.0 license”. The visits name filter uses a modified selection of the LDNOOBW lists ("List of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene, and Otherwise Bad Words"), © 2012–2020 Shutterstock, Inc., licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), without warranties. Changes were made to reduce false positives.

With “Block this person”, you no longer see that person’s name or gifts or receive alerts about their visits. Bees already earned stay; new gifts during the block are ignored and are not recovered after unblocking. Choose “Unblock” in Settings ▸ Visit safety ▸ “Blocked people”. Blocking does not notify the person or automatically send a report. A published garden remains public: other accounts or tools can still access it. Visit alerts are generic, with no name, and may be delayed or absent.

Blocks are kept on your phone, in your private iCloud and in the garden copy. A block takes effect immediately on this iPhone even offline and is updated in iCloud as soon as possible. Restoring an old copy merges blocks with existing ones rather than replacing them. Reinstalling recovers those held in iCloud.

The alert says “Bees have arrived in your garden.”: it contains no names and may be delayed or absent; eligible gifts are counted when you open the app.

4. How to report

“If someone is in danger, call 112 right away.”

In a garden you are visiting, open “…” and choose “Report the name” or “Report the garden”; for a gift in the hive, open its “…” and choose “Report”. For other problems, go to “Settings” ▸ “Visit safety” ▸ “Report a problem”. The same menus offer “Block this person”, with a confirmation; blocking is separate from reporting.

Choose a reason and tap “Prepare the email”. The app prepares an email to info@gusela-solutions.it; check it and press Send in your email app. For an identified name, garden or gift, the subject is “Report · Grow Your Life · GYL-R1 XXXXXXXX” and the body contains the full GYL-R1.… reference, which you should not change. “Report a problem”, without specific content, does not include that reference. The app neither sends automatically nor issues receipts: opening, closing, copying or saving a draft does not confirm sending or delivery.

If Mail has no account or sending fails, use “Open your email app” or “Copy address and report” and paste the text into a new email, including in webmail.

You can also email directly, without the app, iCloud, a subscription or accepting the rules. Include the app’s reference, what you saw, where and when, and why you are reporting it. Without a reference, provide the full link and the exact location of the name or garden. Send only essential attachments, excluding unrelated personal data.

In the report form, choose “Possibly illegal content”, explain why you believe it is illegal and confirm the good-faith statement, which is initially unselected: “I believe in good faith that the information in this report is accurate and complete.” You may omit your name for content involving child sexual abuse, as explained below. Where the person is known, “Also block this person” blocks them immediately even if you do not then send the email.

For content you believe is illegal, include:

  • your name and email address, or those of the reporting organisation;
  • a sufficiently clear explanation of why you believe the content is illegal; you do not need to know a law’s article number;
  • the exact electronic location (full link and app reference, if available), with the information needed to identify the content;
  • “I believe in good faith that the information in this report is accurate and complete.”

For reports concerning child sexual abuse and exploitation offences covered by Articles 3–7 of Directive 2011/93/EU, name and email address are not required as report elements. Email still exposes the sending address: you may use an address that does not reveal your identity. Do not send or forward images of child abuse; provide only the location and necessary information. Without a usable reply address, we cannot send an acknowledgement or outcome.

5. Receipt, decisions and timing

The mailbox sends an automatic acknowledgement of receipt. It is a receipt, not a decision. Thomas checks it and the spam folder daily, personally reviews reports and replies within 24 hours of receipt. If more information is needed, he explains what; the first reply does not necessarily mean the review is complete. Urgent cases take priority.

The acknowledgement comes from the info@gusela-solutions.it mailbox, never from the app. A message in the outbox or drafts is not a receipt.

Thomas may ask for a new name, remove or replace a name, take down a published garden, or temporarily or permanently suspend visits for serious or repeated abuse. He considers content and context, severity, repetition and people’s rights. A report alone does not trigger suspension. Where needed, action also covers names kept in older gifts.

A person receiving a restriction receives reasons in the app and, where a relevant contact is available, by email: content and facts considered, measure, scope and duration, relevant rule or law, any use of automated tools, and how to request a review. The reporter receives the outcome and ways to challenge it at the address provided, without the other person’s private information. We do not normally disclose the reporter’s identity or email to the author.

Where there are grounds to suspect offences threatening life or safety, we contact the competent authorities without delay as required by law. This is not an emergency service: for immediate danger, call 112.

6. Asking for a review

If you think a decision or a report outcome is wrong, write to info@gusela-solutions.it with the reference, what you disagree with and any useful information. You can do this while visits are suspended and without a subscription. Thomas performs a fresh human review and explains the outcome; you can also ask to correct a filter’s rejection of a name. Legal remedies and access to competent authorities or courts remain available.

In the app, find “Ask for a review” in Thomas’s message in “Settings” ▸ “Garden visits”, even while visits are suspended.

7. Single point of contact and privacy

The single point of contact for users and authorities, the European Commission and the European Board for Digital Services, under DSA Articles 11–12, is Thomas Nadalet (Gusela Solutions), info@gusela-solutions.it. Contact languages are Italian and English. Communications receive human attention; this channel does not rely solely on automated replies.

Report files, including correspondence and decisions, are kept for six months after closure; attachments and copies of content for 90 days. Exceptions for obligations or disputes are limited and documented. Blocks, moderation measures, recipients and rights are explained in the privacy policy. These rules supplement the app licence and do not limit mandatory legal rights.
