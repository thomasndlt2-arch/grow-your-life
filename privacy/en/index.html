Privacy policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

This policy covers the Grow Your Life iPhone app and its support, privacy, rules and invitation website. It provides the information required by Article 13 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR). The app’s initial release is planned for the 27 European Union countries.

Your personal garden stays on your iPhone and, if enabled, in your private iCloud copy. Visits and invitations are optional and use separate data, some of it public: this data is not anonymous. There are no ads, sales of data or tracking across apps and websites. Optional analytics are separate from the social features.

1. Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is Thomas Nadalet (Gusela Solutions), Via Antole, 58, 32100 Belluno (BL), Italy, VAT number IT01318470257. For privacy, support and safety: info@gusela-solutions.it. You can also write to the postal address above.

2. Your choices and our legal bases

We distinguish between providing the service you request (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)); your optional consent to analytics (6(1)(a)); legal obligations (6(1)(c)); and our legitimate interest in protecting people, data and the service and preventing abuse (6(1)(f)). For legitimate interests, we take account of your rights, especially if you are a child; you can object as explained below.

You do not need to create an account in the app. You can use your personal garden without analytics, visits or invitations. Data needed for an optional feature is required only if you choose it; without that data, the feature cannot work. iCloud and purchases require Apple’s services. To answer an email or locate reported content, we need a contact address and enough information, subject to the exception for certain child abuse reports explained in the rules.

3. Your personal garden, reminders and sharing

To provide your garden (Article 6(1)(b)), your phone stores themes, activities, plants, diary entries and chosen moods, daily and weekly images and video frames, progress and preferences for language, reminders and how the app addresses you. Garden images are drawn by the app, not taken with your camera. Thomas does not receive this content through ordinary use of the app. It remains until you change or delete it; copies follow the separate arrangements below.

Reminders are local notifications on your phone, kept until delivered, changed or disabled; they do not pass through a server. If you ask to share an image or video, iOS sends it to the app or person you choose (Article 6(1)(b)). That recipient then handles it under their own rules and retention periods: deleting the original does not recall shared copies.

4. Your private iCloud copy and backups

If iCloud is on, the app automatically copies your garden to the private Apple CloudKit database so you can recover it on a new iPhone or after reinstalling (Article 6(1)(b), continuity of the service). The copy includes themes, activities, plants, diary, weekly and video images, preferences, bees, rewards and the local information needed to restore them. It is encrypted in your iCloud account, uses some of your storage and does not pass through a server run by Thomas. The developer’s ordinary management tools do not allow Thomas to read or download it.

The copy also includes blocks and the gift ledger; it excludes the age range and acceptance of the rules, which belong to this phone.

In Settings ▸ “Copy in iCloud”, you can stop updates or delete the copy: switching the feature off does not remove the existing copy. It remains until you delete it through the app or your iPhone’s iCloud settings. “Delete all data” requests its removal, as soon as possible if you are offline. Reinstalling does not itself delete remote data; after a restore, the app checks public profiles and does not automatically recreate revoked ones.

General iPhone backups to iCloud or a computer are separate: they depend on your settings and remain until deleted under Apple’s backup management. Thomas cannot read them. Technical revocation markers, containing no garden content, prevent old copies from restoring deleted data. They remain as long as needed for that purpose, with no preset automatic expiry (legitimate interest in security, Article 6(1)(f)).

5. Invitations, rewards and coupons

Invitations are available from age 13 after accepting the rules and use your iCloud account: one friend means one account. To check invitations and award rewards and offers (Article 6(1)(b)), CloudKit stores a technical profile identifier and generation, code, inviter–invitee connection, up to three opening dates and time zone, qualification, rewards and a coupon reservation. This feature does not collect your name, email, contacts or diary content.

Apple and Thomas can access this data. Shared records in the public database are also readable by the app’s iCloud users authorised to access that database; reward wallets and reservations have tighter access controls. This is not a private exchange limited to friends. Apple checks discount eligibility and redemption; Thomas does not receive payment details.

Foreground openings on three different days count, whether consecutive or not; the offer is presented immediately, without waiting for those days. Data remains for participation and reward recovery, including after reinstalling, until deletion; there is no automatic expiry for inactivity. “Delete all data” removes your profile, connections and rewards. To prevent duplicate allocation (Article 6(1)(f)), a technical reservation for an assigned coupon remains until its batch expires, for no more than six months, and is then removed during batch maintenance. It has no links to your profile or inviter, but CloudKit creator metadata means it is not entirely anonymous. For earlier complete deletion, contact us: the coupon must first be withdrawn from distribution.

6. Visits, gifts and notifications without names

Visits are off initially. From age 13, you can accept the rules and open your garden, choosing a name or nickname you can change. To provide the feature you request (Article 6(1)(b)), the public CloudKit database stores your name, a random garden address, the appearance of plants, animals, decorations and bees, and the update date. It does not publish theme titles, activities, diary entries, notes or personal photos. Anyone with the link can view the garden even without iCloud, as can Thomas, and it can also be found by querying the public database: access is not limited to your friends.

Bringing bees requires iCloud and a name, even if your own garden is closed. Each gift stores the name used at the time, destination garden, date, number of bees, any address for a return visit and creator identifiers/metadata. A first visit can bring ten bees, then one per UTC day per friend. Invitations give rewards, never bees. Gifts are readable by the app’s iCloud users and Thomas and may reveal connections between people. Technical profiles and deletion containers are restricted to their creator.

Apple CloudKit and APNs, Apple’s notification service, process subscriptions and delivery identifiers to tell you about gifts (Article 6(1)(b)). The push signal is silent and contains no name. The app checks the gift, name filter, blocks and suspensions before creating a generic local alert without the visitor’s name. If iOS delays or skips the signal, or checks cannot finish, the alert may not appear: eligible bees are recovered when you open the app. Refusing notifications does not prevent visits. The notification subscription is removed when you close visits; Apple’s technical messages and logs follow the provider’s retention criteria, without a specific period verified by Thomas.

Closing visits removes the published garden and its alerts but keeps earlier gifts, including the name used then, for history and correct bee allocation. Gifts have no automatic expiry. Full deletion removes your profile, garden and gifts you sent, and resets bees on your phone. Records created by others may remain, but links to the deleted garden stop working; you can also request action on data about you in those records. An account-restricted revocation marker remains, without your name or garden address, with the generation and CloudKit metadata, as long as needed to prevent old copies from publishing data again (Article 6(1)(f)).

7. Age ranges and children

During setup, a neutral age-range question offers four equally presented answers, none selected in advance: “Under 13”, “13 to 15”, “16 or older”, “I'd rather not say”. Where available, we also use Apple’s Declared Age Range signal. We do not ask for or store your date of birth. Your phone keeps only the age range and minimum information needed to apply and update protections, until corrected or deleted. We do not publish the age range, send it to analytics or include it in the garden copy. Apple’s signal is an age declaration, not a document-based identity check.

The purpose is to protect children (legitimate interest, Article 6(1)(f)). Under 16, the analytics question is not shown and TelemetryDeck never starts. Under 13, visits and invitations are unavailable. Without a usable age range, all these features stay off; your personal garden still works. If sources disagree, the more protective restriction applies. You can correct your answer in “Settings” ▸ “Privacy” ▸ “Your age”; the app may ask Apple again.

If Apple provides no usable range, your answer applies. With “I'd rather not say”, only what Apple confirms applies; an Apple range spanning age 16 is insufficient to enable analytics. A later refusal to share with Apple does not remove a protection already known. A more protective correction takes effect immediately; one allowing more features requires confirmation and another request to Apple. When Apple indicates a lower range, you can use “Ask Apple again”. The range does not increase automatically over time.

Existing users are asked once after the update. For users under 13, visits are closed and public participation in visits and invitations is revoked; remote removal waits for a connection if necessary. Bees and rewards already earned stay on the phone. Reaching another age range does not automatically switch visits, invitations or analytics consent back on.

Local information includes your answer, what Apple confirms about the ages of 13 and 16, dates, the rule version and a fingerprint of the iCloud account. Changing the iCloud account on the phone prompts the question again and clears your analytics choice. If you had consented to analytics before the update, that consent is held pending the age answer: it applies again from age 16; below 16 or without a usable range, it is cleared.

8. Filtering, blocks, reports and moderation

The app checks names on your phone, without sending them to a filtering service, before publication and display. It uses all six languages’ lists together and also looks for disguised words, links, email addresses, phone numbers and control or invisible characters. It may miss abuse or reject a harmless name: “Choose another name” offers “Ask for a check” to email Thomas. A rejected incoming name appears as “Name not available”, including for VoiceOver; hiding a name does not by itself remove bees from a valid gift. The filter does not check garden designs or replace review of reports.

To protect people and prevent abuse (Article 6(1)(f)), “Block this person” keeps the person’s opaque reference, the already-filtered observed name, date, and block and unblock operations on your phone, in private iCloud and in the garden copy. A record of credited or ignored gifts travels with this data to avoid counting bees twice or recovering blocked gifts. Unblocking does not erase this record or the technical history needed to merge choices across your phones. Manage blocks in “Settings” ▸ “Visit safety” ▸ “Blocked people”. An old copy merges protections with existing ones without overwriting them. Blocking does not notify the person or send a report: it hides their name, garden and gifts and stops alerts about their visits; bees already earned remain. It does not make your public garden private or prevent access through other accounts or tools.

Visits and invitations are available from age 13. Before opening visits or choosing a name, bringing bees for the first time, preparing or sharing an invitation or confirming a code, the app asks you to accept the “Rules for visits and invitations”: “Accept and continue” accepts and continues; “Not now” leaves things as they are. Acceptance covers visits and invitations once per version on this phone. The version, date and point of acceptance stay only on the phone, outside the garden copy: a new version of the rules or another phone requires acceptance again. Reading the rules, reporting, blocking and unblocking do not require acceptance. Your personal garden remains available without visits and invitations.

“Report” prepares an email to info@gusela-solutions.it; you must check and send it. Thomas and the email provider process the email address, any supplied name, reason, explanation, observed content, optional attachments, opaque reference and follow-up correspondence. The reference locates the content and version without including the email or name of the Apple account; it is still linkable data, not anonymous. Reports are not published, and the reporter’s identity is not normally disclosed to the author.

Choose a reason and tap “Prepare the email”. The app prepares an email to info@gusela-solutions.it; check it and press Send in your email app. For an identified name, garden or gift, the subject is “Report · Grow Your Life · GYL-R1 XXXXXXXX” and the body contains the full GYL-R1.… reference, which you should not change. “Report a problem”, without specific content, does not include that reference. The app neither sends automatically nor issues receipts: opening, closing, copying or saving a draft does not confirm sending or delivery.

The acknowledgement comes from the info@gusela-solutions.it mailbox, never from the app. A message in the outbox or drafts is not a receipt.

Thomas personally reviews reports, replies within 24 hours and may remove a name, take down a published garden or suspend visits. The author receives reasons and can request a review. The basis is the legitimate interest in safety (Article 6(1)(f)) and, for activities required by law, compliance with applicable obligations, including illegal-content reports and statements of reasons under DSA Articles 16–18 (GDPR Article 6(1)(c)). Strictly necessary data may be shared with competent authorities or confidential advisers to meet obligations or defend rights.

Reports, correspondence and decisions are kept for six months after closure; copies of content and attachments for 90 days after closure. Identifiers needed to enforce a suspension remain for its duration, reviewed at least every six months. If an obligation, authority request or dispute requires longer retention, we separately keep only what is needed, reviewing the reason and duration. These criteria also cover exported copies and email. How to report and request a review.

9. Anonymous or pseudonymous analytics

Only from age 16 and with your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a) and consent to device access under ePrivacy rules), the app sends counts to TelemetryDeck GmbH, Augsburg, Germany, to understand usage and improve reliability. Without eligibility and consent, the component does not start, send or queue events for later. You can withdraw consent in “Settings” ▸ “Privacy” ▸ “Anonymous statistics”, without losing features. Withdrawal stops transmissions and discards local queues; it does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing.

Counts cover setup, completed activities, opening recaps and the diary, gardens, invitations, visits, offers and subscriptions, with fixed values such as activity type, zone, milestone or plan. Diagnostics may count storage-opening problems without content or file names. We do not send your written text, theme or visit names, titles, notes, diary, activity times, invitation or coupon codes, CloudKit identifiers, email, location or payment details.

The component adds app and system version/build, device model and architecture, screen size/scale/orientation, configured language, region and time zone, appearance and text direction, accessibility settings (text, contrast, motion, transparency, colours), event date/time and periods, App Store/TestFlight/simulator environment, component version, first use, usage days and session counts and durations. Sessions have a random code; launches and sessions are also counted. TelemetryDeck records receipt.

A code derived from the iOS device identifier for the developer is transformed on the phone using SHA-256 and an added value, then transformed again by the provider. TelemetryDeck says it does not retain IP addresses. These measures reduce identifiability, but transforming an identifier alone does not prove anonymity: we therefore describe the analytics as anonymous or pseudonymous. Thomas sees results in the provider’s dashboard; they are not used for advertising or linked with other websites and apps.

Events already sent follow TelemetryDeck’s stated retention: there is no guaranteed deletion schedule; the provider anticipates 7–10 years without guaranteeing this, and account closure does not necessarily remove events. We do not promise automatic deletion after 12 months. You can exercise the rights below for personal data that remains identifiable; genuinely anonymous aggregates cannot be separated into one person’s data. TelemetryDeck privacy FAQ (checked on 14 October 2026).

Apple keeps history and documents under its obligations; Thomas keeps received statements and documents for the period required by applicable accounting and tax law (Article 6(1)(c)). Any purchase analytics remain separate and require consent.

12. The website and GitHub Pages

The static website is hosted by GitHub Pages, with no cookies, analytics or embedded external resources or scripts. GitHub logs IP addresses for security, even without an account, and processes technical request data under its policy. The legitimate interest in providing and protecting the website is the basis for delivery (Article 6(1)(f)); GitHub sets the bases and retention for its own processing in its policy. Thomas does not keep a separate archive of visitor logs. GitHub uses purpose, security and legal-obligation criteria, without a specific Pages log retention period verified here.

In links generated by the app, data after “#” stays in the browser and is not sent to the website server. We also accept older parameters after “?” for compatibility: in that format, they reach GitHub and may enter its logs. The page neither displays nor passes to the app any name in an old link and does not query CloudKit. It reads the code on your device; the app opens only when you tap the link. Your language choice stays in the link, without cookies or persistent storage. External links open services with their own rules only when you follow them.

Recipients are listed for each feature: Apple for iCloud/CloudKit, APNs and the App Store; TelemetryDeck for analytics you accept; GitHub for the website; the email service provider for correspondence and reports; Thomas, any advisers bound by confidentiality, and authorities where necessary. Transfers of personal data require an adequacy decision or suitable safeguards, such as standard clauses and supplementary measures where needed. 14. Deleting data

Settings ▸ “Your data” ▸ “Delete all data” removes your local garden, diary, images, recaps and choices, requests deletion of the private copy and public data described above, and returns the app to setup. Offline CloudKit operations remain pending and resume with the same account. Uninstalling before completion may leave remote data: after reinstalling, use “Delete visits from this iCloud account” or contact support. General Apple backups, shared copies, coupon reservations, technical revocations and necessary case files follow the specific criteria in this policy.

Deletion also removes blocks and the gift ledger from the phone and requests their removal from private iCloud as soon as a connection is available. Another iPhone on the same account that still holds the blocks may restore them through syncing.

Uninstalling alone does not delete the iCloud copy, and none of these actions cancels your subscription: manage it in iPhone Settings ▸ your name ▸ Subscriptions.

15. Your rights

Write to info@gusela-solutions.it to exercise, subject to the GDPR’s conditions:

  • access to your data and a copy;
  • correction of inaccurate data and completion of incomplete data;
  • erasure and restriction of processing;
  • portability of data you provided, in a structured, machine-readable format, for automated processing based on consent or contract, and transfer to another controller where technically possible;
  • objection, on grounds relating to your situation, to processing based on legitimate interests;
  • withdrawal of consent at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing.

We normally reply within one month. If complexity or the number of requests requires up to two additional months, we explain why within the first month. Requests are generally free. We ask only for proportionate information needed to verify your identity and find your data. Never send your Apple password. If we cannot link pseudonymous data to you, we explain this; it is not an automatic refusal of all rights.

You can complain to the Italian data protection authority, the Garante, or the authority in the EEA country where you live, work or believe the infringement occurred, and seek a judicial remedy. You do not need a favourable response from Thomas first.

16. Automated checks and updates

We do not use advertising profiling or solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects within GDPR Article 22. There are automatic checks on age, names and gift validity; Thomas handles report moderation and reviews. You can challenge an error by writing to him. If purposes or this policy change, we publish a dated update and provide the necessary information before new processing begins; where required, we ask for fresh consent.
